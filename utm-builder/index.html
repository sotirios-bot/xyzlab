    What Are UTM Parameters?

    UTM parameters are tags you add to a URL to tell Google Analytics 4 where a visitor came from. When someone clicks a tagged link, GA4 reads the UTM values and records them against the session — so you can see exactly which campaign, channel and source drove each visit, conversion and sale.

    Without UTM parameters, traffic from paid campaigns, emails and social posts can appear as Direct or Referral in GA4, making it impossible to calculate ROAS or cost per acquisition accurately.

    For the full reference on how GA4 uses UTM values to assign channel groups, see Google's campaign parameters documentation.

    UTM Best Practices

    🔡

    Use lowercase only

    UTM values are case-sensitive in GA4. Google and google appear as two separate sources.

    🔗

    Use hyphens, not spaces

    Hyphens keep URLs readable. Spaces become %20 and underscores are harder to scan at a glance.

    🔄

    Be consistent with mediums

    Decide on a convention (e.g. cpc not paid) and stick to it. Inconsistent mediums break GA4 channel grouping.

    🚫

    Don't UTM internal links

    Adding UTMs to links within your own site resets session attribution in GA4. Use only on external links.

    Common UTM Setups by Channel

    Google Search Ads: source=google · medium=cpc · campaign=your-campaign-name · term={keyword}

    Meta Ads (Facebook & Instagram): source=meta · medium=paid-social · campaign=your-campaign-name · content=ad-variant-name

    Email newsletter: source=newsletter · medium=email · campaign=november-2025 · content=cta-top

    Organic social (LinkedIn, Instagram, etc.): source=linkedin · medium=organic-social · campaign=post-topic

    Frequently asked questions on UTM tags

    What is a UTM parameter?
    A UTM parameter is a tag added to a URL that tells Google Analytics 4 where a visitor came from. The five parameters are utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_term and utm_content. When someone clicks a tagged link, GA4 records the values against their session.
    What are the five UTM parameters?
    utm_source (where traffic comes from), utm_medium (the channel type), utm_campaign (the campaign name), utm_term (the paid search keyword) and utm_content (the ad variant). Only the first three are required.
    Do UTM parameters affect SEO?
    UTM parameters do not directly affect your search rankings, but they do affect how Google sees your URLs. If UTM-tagged pages are indexed, it can create duplicate content. Add a canonical tag pointing to the clean URL to prevent this.
    Should UTM values be lowercase?
    Yes. UTM parameter values are case-sensitive in Google Analytics 4. "Google" and "google" are recorded as two separate sources. Always use lowercase and hyphens for readability.
    Can I use UTM parameters on internal links?
    No. Adding UTM parameters to links within your own website resets the session source in GA4, overwriting the original acquisition data. Only use UTMs on external links — in ads, emails and social posts.
    What is the difference between utm_source and utm_medium?
    utm_source is the specific origin of traffic (e.g. google, meta, your-newsletter-name). utm_medium is the category of channel (e.g. cpc, email, organic-social). GA4 uses utm_medium to map sessions to default channel groups like Paid Search and Email.
    How do I track UTM traffic in GA4?
    In GA4, go to Reports > Acquisition > Traffic acquisition. Change the dimension to Session source/medium or Session campaign. All sessions from UTM-tagged links appear here, broken down by the values you used.
    Is this UTM builder free?
    Yes. The UTM builder is completely free and requires no sign-up. Your last 10 generated URLs are saved in your browser's local storage for quick reference.
