    What Are UTM Parameters?

    UTM parameters are tags you add to a URL to tell Google Analytics 4 where a visitor came from. When someone clicks a tagged link, GA4 reads the UTM values and records them against the session — so you can see exactly which campaign, channel and source drove each visit, conversion and sale.

    Without UTM parameters, traffic from paid campaigns, emails and social posts can appear as Direct or Referral in GA4, making it impossible to calculate ROAS or cost per acquisition accurately.

    UTM Parameters Reference

    Parameter Required What it tracks Example
    utm_sourceYesWhere traffic comes fromgoogle, meta, newsletter

    utm_contentNoAd or link variantbanner-blue, cta-header

    UTM Best Practices

    • Use lowercase only. UTM values are case-sensitive in GA4. Google and google appear as two separate sources.
    • Use hyphens, not spaces or underscores. Spaces in URLs become %20 and underscores are harder to scan. Hyphens keep URLs readable.
    • Be consistent with medium values. Decide on a naming convention (e.g. cpc not paid) and stick to it. Inconsistent mediums break channel grouping in GA4.
    • Don't use UTMs on internal links. Adding UTM parameters to links within your own site resets the session source and attribution in GA4 — it only belongs on external links.
    • Use utm_content to A/B test ads. If you have two versions of the same ad, give each a different utm_content value. You'll see which creative drives more conversions in GA4.
    • Store your UTM links. Keep a master log of your tagged URLs so you can maintain naming consistency across campaigns. Use the history below as a quick reference.

    Common UTM Setups by Channel

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is a UTM parameter?
    A UTM parameter is a tag added to a URL that tells Google Analytics 4 where a visitor came from. The five parameters are utm_source (e.g. google), utm_medium (e.g. cpc), utm_campaign (the campaign name), utm_term (keyword) and utm_content (ad variant). When someone clicks a tagged link, GA4 records the values against their session.
    What are the five UTM parameters?
    The five UTM parameters are: utm_source (where traffic comes from, e.g. google or newsletter), utm_medium (the channel type, e.g. cpc or email), utm_campaign (the campaign name), utm_term (the paid search keyword, optional) and utm_content (the ad variant, optional). Only the first three are required.
    Do UTM parameters affect SEO?
    UTM parameters do not directly affect your search rankings, but they do affect how Google sees your URLs. If UTM-tagged pages are indexed, it can create duplicate content. Add a canonical tag pointing to the clean URL, or use the URL parameters tool in Google Search Console to tell Google to ignore them.
    Should UTM values be lowercase?
    Yes. UTM parameter values are case-sensitive in Google Analytics 4. "Google" and "google" are recorded as two separate sources. Always use lowercase and use hyphens instead of spaces or underscores for readability.
    Can I use UTM parameters on internal links?
    No. Adding UTM parameters to links within your own website resets the session source in GA4, overwriting the original acquisition data. Only use UTM parameters on external links — in ads, emails, social posts and other off-site placements.
    What is the difference between utm_source and utm_medium?
    utm_source is the specific origin of traffic (e.g. google, meta, your-newsletter-name). utm_medium is the category of channel (e.g. cpc, email, organic-social). GA4 uses utm_medium to map sessions to default channel groups like Paid Search, Email and Organic Social.
    How do I track UTM traffic in GA4?
    In GA4, go to Reports > Acquisition > Traffic acquisition. Change the dimension to Session source/medium or Session campaign. All sessions from UTM-tagged links appear here, broken down by the values you used.

    Yes. The UTM builder is completely free and requires no sign-up. Your last 10 generated URLs are saved in your browser's local storage for quick reference.
