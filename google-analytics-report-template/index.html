What Is a GA4 Reporting Dashboard?

A GA4 reporting dashboard is a structured summary of your Google Analytics 4 data. Rather than reading a raw export from GA4 each month, the dashboard organises your traffic, engagement and conversion data into a shareable format — with KPI tiles, channel breakdowns and trend charts that make it easy to explain performance to clients, managers or stakeholders.

GA4 changed many of Universal Analytics' core metrics. This template is built for GA4 from scratch — it uses engaged sessions and engagement rate as the primary engagement metrics, not bounce rate.

GA4 Metrics Explained: What to Report and Why

Sessions — total visits. The baseline traffic metric.

Engaged sessions — visits lasting over 10 seconds, with 2+ page views, or with a conversion. This replaced bounce rate as GA4's core engagement signal.

Engagement rate — engaged sessions ÷ sessions. Target: above 50% for most sites.

Key events — what GA4 calls conversions. These are the actions you've marked as important: purchases, form submissions, sign-ups.

Conversion rate — key events ÷ sessions. The most important metric for campaign evaluation.

The template displays all five metrics as KPI tiles and breaks them down by channel in the dashboard view.

What should a good GA4 reporting dashboard include?

A useful GA4 dashboard answers three questions in one view: how much traffic came in, how engaged were those visitors, and how many converted? Here's the structure that answers all three:

  • KPI tiles — Sessions, users, engaged sessions, engagement rate and key events (conversions)
  • Channel breakdown — Sessions and conversion rate by channel group, so you can see where converting traffic is coming from
  • Top landing pages — Which pages are driving the most traffic and engagement
  • MoM trend chart — Sessions and conversions plotted month over month
  • Device breakdown — Desktop vs. mobile sessions and engagement, to inform UX decisions
  • Period comparison — MoM or YoY change clearly shown next to each metric

How to Use the Channel Breakdown Effectively

The channel breakdown shows sessions and conversion rate by default channel group. The most useful question it answers: which channels are sending converting traffic, and which are sending volume without results? A channel with 10% of sessions but 25% of conversions is punching above its weight — worth investing more in. A channel with 40% of sessions but 5% of conversions needs either better targeting or a landing page fix.

The template flags channels where conversion rate is below account average with conditional formatting, so you don't have to scan for underperformers manually.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a GA4 reporting dashboard?
A GA4 reporting dashboard is a summary view of your Google Analytics 4 data. It aggregates traffic, engagement and conversion data into KPI tiles, channel breakdowns and trend charts — giving you a shareable report without manually building charts each month.
What GA4 metrics does the template cover?
The template covers sessions, users, new users, engaged sessions, engagement rate, average engagement time, key events (conversions), conversion rate, and channel-level breakdowns — the core GA4 reporting metrics.
What is the difference between GA4 sessions and engaged sessions?
Sessions is the total number of visits to your site. Engaged sessions is the subset that lasted more than 10 seconds, included a conversion event, or included 2 or more page views. Engaged sessions replaced bounce rate as the primary engagement metric in GA4.
How do I export data from GA4 for the template?
In GA4, go to Reports > Acquisition > Traffic acquisition. Set your date range, then click the download icon (top right of the report) and choose Download CSV. The template's Raw Data tab is formatted to match a standard GA4 traffic acquisition export.
Can I report on GA4 conversions in the template?
Yes. The Conversions tab tracks your key events (conversions in GA4 terminology) by channel and by event name. You can see which channels drive the most conversions and which conversion events are performing best.
Does the template include channel breakdowns?
Yes. The dashboard includes a channel breakdown chart showing sessions and conversion rate by default channel group: Organic Search, Direct, Paid Search, Organic Social, Paid Social, Email, Referral and AI Assistants (if you've set up the custom channel group).
What is a good engagement rate in GA4?
A commonly cited benchmark is 50–70% engaged sessions, but it varies significantly by industry, traffic source and page type. Blog content from social media often has a lower engagement rate than navigational or branded traffic. Compare your own channels against each other first.
Can I use this template for client reporting?
Yes. The cover tab supports a client name and logo. Share view-only access via Google Sheets or export as PDF. The dashboard is designed to be sent directly to clients without additional formatting.
