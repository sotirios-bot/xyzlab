What Is a Google Ads Reporting Dashboard?

A Google Ads reporting dashboard is a structured view of your campaign performance data. Instead of reading a raw data export, the dashboard surfaces the metrics that matter — ROAS, CPA, impression share, CTR — with visual indicators that flag problems and highlight what's working.

This template is built for monthly reporting cycles: export once, paste once, and your dashboard is ready to review or share.

Which Google Ads Metrics Should You Include in a Report?

For Search campaigns, the core metrics are impressions, clicks, CTR, average CPC, conversions, conversion rate, CPA and impression share. For Shopping and Performance Max, replace impression share with ROAS and add revenue if you're tracking e-commerce. The template includes all of these across tabs, with the most important KPIs on the dashboard front page.

How to Use the Template (Step by Step)

1

Export your Google Ads data

In Google Ads, go to Reports and download a campaign-level report as a CSV. Include the columns that match the Raw Data tab headers in the template.

4

Review and share

Switch to the Dashboard tab to see KPIs and campaign performance. Share via Google Sheets or export as PDF for client delivery.

What should a good Google Ads dashboard include?

A well-designed Google Ads dashboard does more than display raw numbers — it shows context, flags anomalies and makes it easy to explain performance without a 20-minute walkthrough. Here's what to include:

  • KPI tiles — Total spend, ROAS, CPA and conversions with period-over-period change
  • Campaign performance table — One row per campaign with all key metrics and colour-coded target tracking
  • Trend chart — Spend and conversions plotted over time to spot peaks and dips
  • Quality Score tracker — Average score by campaign so you can identify where ad relevance or landing pages need work
  • Device and audience breakdown — Where your budget is going and what's converting
  • Targets vs. actuals — Clear indicators showing whether campaigns are on track against your CPA or ROAS goals

For more on setting up Google Ads reports, see the official Google Ads reporting overview.

Why Track Quality Score in a Reporting Dashboard?

Quality Score is a 1–10 score Google assigns to your keywords based on expected CTR, ad relevance and landing page experience. A low Quality Score increases your CPC and reduces your ad rank — meaning you pay more for fewer impressions. Tracking Quality Score monthly helps you identify keywords or campaigns where improving ad copy or landing pages could reduce costs without increasing bids. The Quality Score tab in this template logs your average score by campaign so you can spot trends over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Google Ads reporting dashboard?
A Google Ads reporting dashboard is a visual summary of your campaign performance. It pulls together spend, ROAS, CPA, CTR and conversion data into one view, so you can communicate performance clearly without manually building charts.
What metrics does the template include?
The template covers spend, impressions, clicks, CTR, average CPC, conversions, conversion rate, CPA (cost per conversion), ROAS and impression share — the core metrics for Search, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns.

Yes. The campaign table separates campaigns by type, so you can compare Search, Shopping and Performance Max side by side. Some metrics — like search impression share — only populate for Search campaigns.
Can I use this for client reporting?
Yes. The cover tab is designed for agency use — add your client's name, logo and date range, then share view-only access via Google Sheets, or export a PDF. The design is clean enough to send to a client directly.
How is this different from the Google Ads Report Template?
The Report Template is a lightweight export-and-share sheet focused on campaign-level numbers. The Reporting Dashboard includes KPI tiles, conditional formatting, trend charts and a Quality Score tracker — it's the more complete version for regular monthly reporting.
What is a good CTR for Google Ads?
Average CTR for Google Search ads sits around 3–5% across industries, but branded campaigns typically run 10–30%+ and non-brand can be 1–3%. The template shows your CTR in context — against your own account average and against the target you set.
Does the template include a Quality Score tracker?
Yes. The Quality Score tab logs your average Quality Score by campaign and over time. Quality Score affects CPCs and ad rank, so tracking it monthly helps you spot where ad relevance or landing pages are dragging down performance.
